# Long-form script: Claude made a boss fight for my robot (21 Nov)

16:9, about 6 minutes. One ElevenLabs file per line, named 01.mp3 to 21.mp3.

| # | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Teaser 16:9 full screen | This boss fight was made entirely with code. No game engine. No video editor. Claude Code wrote every frame and every sound, and then it marked its own homework. |
| 2 | Freeze on round 1 score table, "3" highlighted | And its first attempt scored a three out of ten. Here's how it got to eight. |
| 3 | Bit intro card | I'm Amit, a systems engineer learning AI in public. This is Bit, my robot presenter. And everything on this channel is made with code. |
| 4 | Search trend numbers, generic "one prompt" result mock | Right now, Claude motion graphics is exploding on YouTube. People post incredible videos and say it took one prompt. Then you try it, and you get a gradient, some centred text, and not much else. |
| 5 | "10% prompt / 90% setup" slide | The best explanation I found: the prompt is only ten percent of the result. The other ninety percent is the setup around it. So I tested that, on my own robot. |
| 6 | BRIEF.md scrolling, sections highlighted | I wrote a director's brief. It's one page, with twelve short sections. The film in one line. References. The tools it's allowed to use. A character bible. A beat sheet. Every word allowed on screen. And rules for checking its own work. |
| 7 | Character bible: Bit and Professor turnaround | The character bible is the part that matters most. Bit's exact colours, his proportions, five expressions, and how he moves. Without it, the AI redraws your character differently in every shot. |
| 8 | Gates diagram | I also added gates. At each one, Claude has to stop and show me before it carries on. It's like design reviews on an engineering project. You don't wait until the end to find out it's wrong. |
| 9 | Gate 1 recording, sped up | First surprise. My Mac had none of the tools it needed. So before drawing anything, Claude installed them itself. Eight minutes later, it showed me three style frames. |
| 10 | The 3 style frames, problems circled | And honestly, they looked like a real game. But I found four problems. The boss bar sat right where YouTube puts its buttons. Two words were hard to read. Debris covered the winning text. And the title card was half empty. |
| 11 | Gate 2 beat grid, beat markers pulsing | Gate two was the beat grid. The music is a chiptune Claude wrote in code, at a hundred and twenty beats per minute. Every jump, every hit and every combo sits on a beat, down to the exact frame. |
| 12 | Gate 3 recording, first render | Gate three, the first full render. Six hundred frames, exactly twenty seconds. It even caught and fixed its own crash before showing me. |
| 13 | Six criteria list | Then the critique loop. Claude pulls one frame for every beat and scores the film out of ten on six things: the hook, readability, beat sync, the characters, game feel and the ending. |
| 14 | Round 1 table; black opening clip | Round one. Beat sync, eight. Everything else, six or seven. And the hook? Three. Because the first two seconds were a black screen, and on Shorts, that's exactly when people swipe away. |
| 15 | Amit's notes on screen | So I added my own notes as a viewer. Open on the most exciting moment. Make Bit bigger. And stop the boss card covering him. |
| 16 | Round 2 bar 8 clip | Round two. Claude restaged the whole fight. The Professor throws a word on every beat, and Bit shoots each one out of the air. FAST becomes within two hundred milliseconds. TBD becomes ten megabytes per second. |
| 17 | Score table animating 3 → 8 | The hook went from three to eight. Every score, eight or more. So the loop stopped, after two rounds out of four. |
| 18 | Final pass recording; 3 ms → 12 ms | And one last thing it caught on its own. In the final check, the sound in the first second came from the wrong part of the film. It noticed because the timing error jumped from three milliseconds to twelve. That's the kind of check I'd want on a real project. |
| 19 | Three lessons slide | So, what did I learn? One: structure beats a long prompt. Two: a character bible keeps your character on-model. Three: gates and a scoring loop turn "looks okay" into something you can actually measure. |
